# Annotated Manuscript: Rohmann et al. (2024)

### 1. Peer-Reviewed Original Research Confirmation
This article was published in *Personality and Individual Differences* (Volume 218), a double-blind, peer-reviewed journal. It reports original quantitative empirical research analyzing primary survey data ($N = 149$).

### 2. The Gap the Authors Claim
The authors claim that while previous research established a link between grandiose narcissism and internet trolling, existing literature failed to account for how distinct sub-dimensions of narcissism (specifically vulnerable narcissism) independently predict trolling behavior, as well as how self-esteem functions as a protective moderating buffer.

### 3. The Theoretical Framework
The study uses the **3D Model of Narcissism** (distinguishing agentic extraversion, antagonistic self-protection, and neurotic introversion) alongside **Self-Esteem Moderation Theory** as its named theoretical framework.

### 4. The Method
* **Unit of Analysis:** Individual online commentators/internet users.
* **Sample:** $N = 149$ participants.
* **Measurement of Key Constructs:** Measured using established quantitative Likert scales, including the German Global Assessment of Internet Trolling Revised (GAIT-R-G) for trolling behavior, the Buss & Perry Aggression Questionnaire (AQ) for trait aggression, the Rosenberg Self-Esteem Scale (RSES) for global self-esteem, and the G-NPI-13 & NI-R-36 for narcissistic traits.

### 5. The Finding
In a sample of $N = 149$, vulnerable narcissism emerged as a significant positive predictor of internet trolling ($r_{\text{partial}} = .28, p = .001$), whereas grandiose narcissism had no significant independent effect once vulnerable traits were controlled, and global self-esteem significantly moderated this relationship by dampening the effect of narcissism on trolling behavior.

### 6. Limitations
* **Admitted Limitation:** The authors explicitly admit that the cross-sectional survey design prevents establishing direct causal relationships between narcissistic traits, self-esteem, and trolling behavior over time.
* **Unadmitted Limitation:** The reliance on self-report instruments for socially undesirable behaviors creates a significant risk of social desirability bias, as active internet trolls are unlikely to accurately or honestly self-report their antagonistic online conduct.

### 7. Connection Statement
This article provides my project with an empirical foundation for operationalizing online trolling as an expression of vulnerable narcissism and low self-esteem rather than simple status-seeking or extraverted aggression. Methodologically, it offers validated, peer-reviewed measurement instruments (such as the GAIT-R-G) that can be adapted for measuring user aggression and antagonistic commentary across digital platforms. What it leaves for my project to do is examine how specific platform-level architectural features (such as algorithmic feeds, comment threading, and moderation systems) interact with these psychological vulnerabilities, as Rohmann et al. focused solely on individual-level traits rather than platform affordances.
