# Topic Selection and Research Questions

### 1. Topic Statement
This study will examine the actions of online gamers through social channels and focus on perceived toxicity and other traits such as narcissism. The target population will consist of PC gamers in specific gaming communities such as Marvel Rivals, Overwatch and Team Fortress 2. All team based multiplayer shooter titles that hold similar audiences.

---

### 2. Research Questions & Hypotheses
1. **RQ1:** How do competitive online gamers describe the primary communication behaviors and social triggers that escalate match tension into verbal toxicity during ranked play?
2. **H1:** Higher perceived quality and clarity of team voice communication in competitive matches is negatively associated with reported instances of player toxicity.
3. **H2:** Exposure to high levels of post-match player toxicity is negatively associated with a player’s likelihood of continuing a gaming session (session retention).

---

### 3. Why That Form
RQ1 is structured as an exploratory research question because the qualitative nuances and subjective triggers of in-game toxicity lack a unified, single-direction theoretical model. H1 and H2 are stated as directional hypotheses because Social Exchange Theory and Deindividuation Theory predict clear behavioral outcomes when communication quality shifts and negative social interactions occur in anonymous, high-stakes environments. Specifically, H1 predicts that positive voice comms reduce antisocial behavior, while H2 predicts an inverse relationship between toxicity exposure and continued gameplay engagement.

---

### 4. Variable Preview

| Research Question / Hypothesis | What Would Be Measured | Unit of Measurement |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **RQ1** | Qualitative triggers and types of verbal toxicity (e.g., identity-based slurs, performance blame, griefing) | Categorical communication themes (coded interview responses) |
| **H1** | Perceived voice communication quality; reported frequency of in-game toxicity | 5-point Likert scale (1 = Very Poor, 5 = Excellent); Reported incidents per 5 matches |
| **H2** | Self-reported toxicity exposure; gaming session duration post-match | 5-point Likert scale; Intent-to-play duration in minutes/matches |

---

### 5. Justification
Online multiplayer gaming has always had forms of toxicity running rampant for a multitude of combined reasons, from player age and maturity to game topic or type. We can use common tropes to understand where toxicity may occur, but the deeper question is: why does it stick around, and in large numbers? Unchecked behavior can contribute to player burnout, a fragmented community, and reduced long-term player retention. If we can identify the dynamics in communication and the thresholds that mitigate toxic patterns of behavior in competitive spaces, the research can provide practical insights for better in-game moderation, with opt-in communication features being redesigned to create a safer digital gaming environment.
---

### 6. Five-Criteria Check

#### Question 1 (RQ1)
* **Criterion 1 (Specific):** Specifies North American competitive PC gamers, specific communication channels (voice/text), and qualitative toxicity triggers.
* **Criterion 2 (Measurable):** Operationalized through thematic coding of semi-structured player interviews.
* **Criterion 3 (Answerable):** Feasible within a semester timeline using a qualitative sample of 10–12 active competitive players.
* **Criterion 4 (Not already answered):** While online harassment is broadly documented, specific qualitative trigger patterns within contemporary ranked matchmaking remain actively debated in communication literature.

#### Hypothesis 1 (H1)
* **Criterion 1 (Specific):** Bounded to ranked multiplayer matchmaking, evaluating structured team voice comms against perceived toxicity levels.
* **Criterion 2 (Measurable):** Measured via standard Likert ratings for communication quality and self-reported incident counts.
* **Criterion 3 (Answerable):** Easily collected via a brief quantitative survey distributed across online gaming communities over 2 weeks.
* **Criterion 4 (Not already answered):** Empirical links between structured voice callouts and toxicity reduction in modern hero/tactical shooters present an open empirical question.

#### Hypothesis 2 (H2)
* **Criterion 1 (Specific):** Directs focus to post-match toxicity exposure and player session-quit behaviors.
* **Criterion 2 (Measurable):** Measured using self-reported toxicity severity scales against session duration estimates.
* **Criterion 3 (Answerable):** Fully answerable through a single quantitative survey questionnaire.
* **Criterion 4 (Not already answered):** Measuring the direct statistical impact of single-session toxicity on immediate session termination offers actionable, unverified data for game studies literature.
